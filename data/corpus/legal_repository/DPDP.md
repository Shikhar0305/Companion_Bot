### DPDP Act, 2023 Section 4 — Grounds for processing personal data

**Section Number:** 4
**Section Name:** Grounds for processing personal data

**Description:**
Permits processing of personal data only for a lawful purpose with the data principal's consent or for certain legitimate uses — establishing that processing without a lawful basis is impermissible.

**Applicability:**
Loan-app and data-misuse cases where personal data (contacts, photos, identifiers) is processed without a lawful basis.

**Evidence Requirements:**
App permission records, consent artefacts (or absence thereof), data-flow evidence, and scope-of-processing documentation.

**Related Crime Categories:** Loan App Fraud

**Investigator Notes:**
DPDP is primarily a civil/regulatory regime (enforced by the Data Protection Board), not a criminal statute — use it alongside IT Act/BNS criminal charges, not as a substitute.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Expecting criminal penalties directly under DPDP; not documenting the absence of valid consent.

### DPDP Act, 2023 Section 5 & 6 — Notice and consent

**Section Number:** 5 & 6
**Section Name:** Notice and consent

**Description:**
Requires a clear notice and free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous consent (with the right to withdraw) before processing personal data.

**Applicability:**
Loan apps and services that harvest data via coerced, bundled or non-transparent consent.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Consent screens/records, notice text, permission scopes, and evidence of bundled/coerced consent.

**Related Crime Categories:** Loan App Fraud

**Investigator Notes:**
Bundled or coerced 'consent' to excessive permissions is not valid consent — evidences unlawful processing.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Accepting a generic permission grant as valid DPDP consent; not preserving the consent UI/flow.

### DPDP Act, 2023 Section 8 — General obligations of a data fiduciary (incl. purpose limitation and data minimisation)

**Section Number:** 8
**Section Name:** General obligations of a data fiduciary (incl. purpose limitation and data minimisation)

**Description:**
Imposes obligations on data fiduciaries including processing only as necessary for the stated purpose, ensuring accuracy, security safeguards, and erasure when the purpose is served.

**Applicability:**
Excessive data collection (e.g., loan apps accessing contacts/gallery beyond need) and failure to safeguard data.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Comparison of data collected vs purpose, security-control records, and retention/erasure practices.

**Related Crime Categories:** Loan App Fraud

**Investigator Notes:**
Excessive collection beyond the stated purpose breaches S.8 — a key indicator of malicious loan-app behaviour.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Not benchmarking collected data against the stated purpose; ignoring retention/erasure failures.

### DPDP Act, 2023 Section 33 — Penalties and the Data Protection Board

**Section Number:** 33
**Section Name:** Penalties and the Data Protection Board

**Description:**
Empowers the Data Protection Board to inquire into breaches and impose financial penalties (per the Schedule) for non-compliance.

**Applicability:**
Regulatory escalation track for data-protection breaches identified during cyber investigations.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Documented breach, affected data principals, and the fiduciary's non-compliance.

**Related Crime Categories:** Loan App Fraud

**Investigator Notes:**
Route DPDP breaches to the Data Protection Board for penalties; keep the criminal case under IT Act/BNS in parallel.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Treating DPDP penalties as criminal sentencing; failing to refer the regulatory aspect to the Board.
