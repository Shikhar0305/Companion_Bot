### BNSS, 2023 Section 94 — Summons to produce document or other thing (incl. electronic records)

**Section Number:** 94
**Section Name:** Summons to produce document or other thing (incl. electronic records)

**Description:**
Empowers a Court or officer in charge of a police station to require, by summons/written order, the production of any document or electronic record necessary for an investigation, inquiry or trial.

**Applicability:**
The primary tool to obtain bank records, KYC, CDRs, IP/login logs, platform data and subscriber details from third parties.

**Evidence Requirements:**
The production order, the producing entity's certificate/authentication, and a clear chain of custody for received records.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Use S.94 production orders early and specifically (define exact data fields and date ranges). Pair with preservation requests so data is not purged before production.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Vague/over-broad requests that return unusable data; requesting production after retention windows expire; missing the authentication certificate for received records.

### BNSS, 2023 Section 173 — Information in cognizable cases (FIR registration)

**Section Number:** 173
**Section Name:** Information in cognizable cases (FIR registration)

**Description:**
Mandates registration of information relating to a cognizable offence irrespective of the area where it is committed (zero-FIR), and enables information to be given electronically.

**Applicability:**
FIR registration for every cognizable cyber-crime; supports zero-FIR and e-FIR for cross-jurisdiction complaints.

**Evidence Requirements:**
The FIR, the complainant's information, and the NCRP/1930 complaint reference where applicable.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Cyber-crime is inherently cross-jurisdictional — use zero-FIR rather than refusing on territorial grounds; capture the NCRP acknowledgement.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Refusing/redirecting complaints on jurisdiction grounds instead of registering a zero-FIR; delaying FIR and losing the freeze/recovery window.

### BNSS, 2023 Section 176 — Procedure for investigation of cognizable offence

**Section Number:** 176
**Section Name:** Procedure for investigation of cognizable offence

**Description:**
Empowers the officer in charge to investigate a cognizable case; for offences punishable by seven years or more, requires forensic-expert examination of the crime scene and audio-video recording of evidence collection.

**Applicability:**
Governs the conduct of cyber-crime investigations, including mandatory forensic involvement for serious offences.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Case diary, forensic examination records, and audio-video documentation of search/seizure where mandated.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
For offences ≥7 years, the S.176(3) forensic and videography requirements are mandatory — plan forensic support before search/seizure.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Skipping mandatory forensic/videography steps for serious offences (risking inadmissibility); poor case-diary discipline.

### BNSS, 2023 Section 185 — Search by police officer

**Section Number:** 185
**Section Name:** Search by police officer

**Description:**
Empowers an investigating officer with reasonable grounds to conduct a search, recording the grounds and the things to be searched for, with the search conducted/witnessed per prescribed safeguards.

**Applicability:**
Search of premises, devices and digital storage during cyber-crime investigation.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Recorded grounds of search, search memo, independent witnesses, seizure list, and audio-video record of the search.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Record reasonable grounds in writing before the search; use independent witnesses and videography to protect admissibility of seized digital evidence.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Conducting searches without recording grounds or witnesses; seizing devices without hashing/imaging and a contemporaneous seizure memo.

### BNSS, 2023 Section 186 — Search/requisition by an investigating officer (incl. beyond local limits) and seizure

**Section Number:** 186
**Section Name:** Search/requisition by an investigating officer (incl. beyond local limits) and seizure

**Description:**
Enables an investigating officer (not below sub-inspector) to cause search/seizure, including through requisition where the place to be searched is beyond the officer's local jurisdiction.

**Applicability:**
Seizure of property/documents and cross-jurisdiction searches — important for cyber-crime spanning multiple districts/states.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Requisition records, seizure memo, chain of custody, and inter-jurisdiction coordination documentation.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Use for lawful cross-jurisdiction seizure; coordinate with the local police station having territorial jurisdiction.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Conducting out-of-jurisdiction seizure without proper requisition/coordination; gaps in chain of custody during transfer.

### BNSS, 2023 Section 193 — Report of police officer on completion of investigation (charge sheet)

**Section Number:** 193
**Section Name:** Report of police officer on completion of investigation (charge sheet)

**Description:**
Requires investigation to be completed without unnecessary delay and governs the police report (charge sheet / final report) forwarded to the Magistrate on completion, including supplementary reports.

**Applicability:**
Filing of the charge sheet for every prosecuted cyber-crime, with documentary, digital and expert evidence annexed.

**Evidence Requirements:**
The S.193 police report with annexures: bank/transaction records, forensic reports, BSA S.63 certificates, and the evidence-correlation matrix.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Ensure all electronic evidence carries BSA S.63 certification before charge-sheeting; use supplementary reports for evidence arriving later (e.g., foreign/exchange data).

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Filing without S.63 certificates for electronic evidence; incomplete fund-trail/attribution; delay beyond statutory timelines.
