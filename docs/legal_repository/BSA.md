### BSA, 2023 Section 2(1)(d) — Definition of "document" (including electronic and digital records)

**Section Number:** 2(1)(d)
**Section Name:** Definition of "document" (including electronic and digital records)

**Description:**
Defines 'document' to expressly include electronic and digital records, bringing emails, messages, server logs, and other digital artefacts within the meaning of documentary evidence.

**Applicability:**
Foundational definition that makes all digital artefacts in cyber cases 'documents' for evidentiary purposes.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Properly identified and preserved electronic records; metadata; source-device/system identification.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
This definition underpins admissibility of all digital evidence under the BSA; cite it when introducing any electronic record.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Treating digital artefacts as informal exhibits rather than 'documents'; failing to identify the source system.

### BSA, 2023 Section 39 — Opinion of experts (incl. digital/forensic experts)

**Section Number:** 39
**Section Name:** Opinion of experts (incl. digital/forensic experts)

**Description:**
Makes the opinion of persons specially skilled in relevant fields (including examiners of electronic/digital evidence) relevant and admissible.

**Applicability:**
Admission of digital forensic reports, malware analysis, blockchain tracing and media-authentication (deepfake) opinions.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Qualified expert's report, the expert's credentials, methodology, and the tools/validation used.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Establish the expert's qualifications and a reproducible methodology; the opinion's weight depends on documented method and integrity.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Submitting forensic conclusions without a qualified expert or documented methodology; tool output without validation.

### BSA, 2023 Section 57 — Proof of electronic and digital records / primary evidence

**Section Number:** 57
**Section Name:** Proof of electronic and digital records / primary evidence

**Description:**
Provides for what constitutes primary evidence, including provisions relevant to electronic and digital records, establishing how such records are proved.

**Applicability:**
Proving the contents of electronic records in cyber prosecutions.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Primary/source electronic records where available; otherwise certified copies under S.63.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Prefer primary/source records; where only secondary copies exist, route admissibility through S.63 certification.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Relying on screenshots/printouts without satisfying primary-evidence or S.63 requirements.

### BSA, 2023 Section 63 — Admissibility of electronic records (certificate requirement)

**Section Number:** 63
**Section Name:** Admissibility of electronic records (certificate requirement)

**Description:**
Governs the admissibility of electronic records and requires a certificate (S.63(4)) identifying the record and the device/process that produced it, signed by the person in charge and an expert, for such records to be admissible.

**Applicability:**
The decisive admissibility gate for ALL electronic evidence — bank logs, CDRs, chats, server logs, forensic images.

**Evidence Requirements:**
The electronic record plus a complete S.63(4) certificate; hash values; description of the producing device/process.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
No electronic evidence should be charge-sheeted without a valid S.63 certificate (the successor to IT Act/Evidence Act §65B). Obtain the certificate at the time of collection.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Missing or defective S.63 certificate (the single most common cause of digital evidence being thrown out); obtaining the certificate as an afterthought.
