### BNS, 2023 Section 61 — Criminal conspiracy

**Section Number:** 61
**Section Name:** Criminal conspiracy

**Description:**
Defines criminal conspiracy as an agreement between two or more persons to do, or cause to be done, an illegal act or a legal act by illegal means, and provides for its punishment.

**Applicability:**
Applies to virtually every organised cyber-crime with multiple actors — mule networks, call-centre scams, syndicates, money-laundering chains.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Evidence of agreement/coordination: chat groups, call records, shared infrastructure, fund-flow linkages, role division among accused.

**Related Crime Categories:** UPI/Banking Fraud, Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, CSAM, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Conspiracy can be inferred from concerted conduct; map each accused's role and the common object. Critical for charging absconding/foreign co-conspirators.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Alleging conspiracy without evidence of a meeting of minds; failing to connect mules/operators into a single agreement; ignoring digital coordination evidence.

### BNS, 2023 Section 74–79 — Sexual harassment, voyeurism, stalking and related offences against women

**Section Number:** 74–79
**Section Name:** Sexual harassment, voyeurism, stalking and related offences against women

**Description:**
Cluster of provisions covering assault/criminal force to outrage modesty, sexual harassment, voyeurism, and stalking (including following/contacting and monitoring electronic communication).

**Applicability:**
Cyber-stalking, online sexual harassment, voyeurism and tech-facilitated stalking; applied in sextortion, impersonation, deepfake and stalking cases.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Persistent communication logs, monitoring/tracking evidence, the offending content, victim statement, and pattern-of-conduct timeline.

**Related Crime Categories:** Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud

**Investigator Notes:**
S.78 expressly covers stalking via monitoring of electronic communication. Build a chronological pattern to show persistence.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Treating a single incident as stalking without showing a course of conduct; failing to preserve the full communication timeline.

### BNS, 2023 Section 303(2) — Theft (including theft of digital assets/data)

**Section Number:** 303(2)
**Section Name:** Theft (including theft of digital assets/data)

**Description:**
Defines theft and prescribes punishment; S.303(2) is the punishment limb. Applied to dishonest taking of movable property, read in the SOP to cover theft of digital assets/data.

**Applicability:**
Where digital assets, funds or data are dishonestly taken (crypto theft, data exfiltration, asset siphoning).

**Evidence Requirements:**
Certified electronic records under BSA S.63 with S.63(4) certificate; hash values (SHA-256); chain of custody; forensic image of device/server; preservation of original media. Proof of dishonest taking without consent, ownership of the asset/data, and the transfer trail.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Loan App Fraud

**Investigator Notes:**
Use where 'taking' is established; for deception-led transfers, cheating provisions (S.318/319) usually fit better.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Confusing theft (taking) with cheating (inducement); failing to prove ownership and dishonest intent.

### BNS, 2023 Section 308 — Extortion

**Section Number:** 308
**Section Name:** Extortion

**Description:**
Defines extortion — intentionally putting a person in fear of injury and thereby dishonestly inducing delivery of property, valuable security or anything that may be converted into a valuable security — and prescribes punishment.

**Applicability:**
Sextortion, ransomware ransom demands, loan-app recovery threats, and any threat-induced payment.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Threat communications, the demand, the fear induced, payment trail, and linkage to the accused.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, CSAM, Dark Web Crime

**Investigator Notes:**
Establish the causal chain: threat → fear → dishonest delivery. Preserve the original threat messages and the payment evidence together.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Proving a threat but not the resulting delivery/attempt; not linking the demanded account to the accused.

### BNS, 2023 Section 316 — Criminal breach of trust

**Section Number:** 316
**Section Name:** Criminal breach of trust

**Description:**
Punishes dishonest misappropriation or conversion of property entrusted to a person, or use/disposal in violation of a direction of law or legal contract governing the trust.

**Applicability:**
Investment/trading scams and advisory frauds where funds entrusted for a stated purpose are misappropriated.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Proof of entrustment, the agreed purpose, the dishonest misappropriation, and the diverted fund trail.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Hinges on 'entrustment'. Distinguish from cheating, where dishonest intent exists at inception.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Charging breach of trust where there was no genuine entrustment; failing to document the agreed purpose of funds.

### BNS, 2023 Section 318 — Cheating

**Section Number:** 318
**Section Name:** Cheating

**Description:**
Defines cheating — fraudulently or dishonestly deceiving a person to deliver property or to do/omit an act causing harm — with graded punishment (S.318(4) being the aggravated limb).

**Applicability:**
The principal fraud provision across nearly all financial cyber-crimes: UPI fraud, investment/trading scams, online-shopping fraud, BEC, job scams, gambling fraud.

**Evidence Requirements:**
The deception, dishonest inducement, the victim's delivery/act, resulting loss, and the money trail to the accused/beneficiary.

**Related Crime Categories:** UPI/Banking Fraud, Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Prove deception AND dishonest inducement AND delivery/harm. Pair with IT Act S.66D for the electronic personation element.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Treating mere non-performance of a contract as cheating without dishonest intent at inception; weak beneficiary-account linkage.

### BNS, 2023 Section 319 — Cheating by personation

**Section Number:** 319
**Section Name:** Cheating by personation

**Description:**
Punishes cheating by pretending to be some other person, or knowingly substituting one person for another, or representing that one is a person other than they really are.

**Applicability:**
Impersonation-led fraud: fake officials (digital arrest), fake sellers/recruiters, executive/vendor impersonation (BEC), and fake profiles.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Evidence of the false persona, the impersonation channel, the deceived victim, and the resulting transfer/act.

**Related Crime Categories:** UPI/Banking Fraud, Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Loan App Fraud, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Deepfake Fraud, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, Dark Web Crime, Online Gambling Fraud, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Distinct from S.66C (misuse of an identifier): S.319 targets the act of pretending to be another person. Often charged together.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Failing to distinguish personation (pretending to be someone) from identity-feature misuse; not preserving the impersonation content.

### BNS, 2023 Section 335 — Making a false document

**Section Number:** 335
**Section Name:** Making a false document

**Description:**
Defines the making of a false document/false electronic record (the foundational act underlying forgery offences).

**Applicability:**
Cited in the SOP (Chapter 1) for fabrication of electronic records used in banking fraud; foundational to the forgery cluster (S.336–340).

**Evidence Requirements:**
Certified electronic records under BSA S.63 with S.63(4) certificate; hash values (SHA-256); chain of custody; forensic image of device/server; preservation of original media. The false document/electronic record, proof of its falsity, and authorship/attribution.

**Related Crime Categories:** UPI/Banking Fraud

**Investigator Notes:**
Read with S.336 (forgery) and S.340 (using a forged document as genuine). Some SOP chapters cite S.336 directly for 'forgery'.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Confusing the definitional provision with the punishment provisions; failing to prove the document is actually false.

### BNS, 2023 Section 336 — Forgery

**Section Number:** 336
**Section Name:** Forgery

**Description:**
Punishes making a false document or false electronic record with intent to cause damage/injury, to support a claim/title, to cause parting with property, or to commit fraud.

**Applicability:**
Forged KYC, fake offer letters/invoices, fabricated screenshots/dashboards, spoofed communications and forged electronic records across fraud categories.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Certified electronic records under BSA S.63 with S.63(4) certificate; hash values (SHA-256); chain of custody; forensic image of device/server; preservation of original media. The forged record, proof of falsity, fraudulent intent, and authorship attribution.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Deepfake Fraud, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, Dark Web Crime, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Forgery of electronic records is squarely covered. Pair with S.340 when the forged record is used as genuine.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Not establishing the specific fraudulent intent required; failing to forensically prove the record was fabricated/altered.

### BNS, 2023 Section 340 — Forged document or electronic record used as genuine

**Section Number:** 340
**Section Name:** Forged document or electronic record used as genuine

**Description:**
Punishes fraudulently or dishonestly using as genuine a document or electronic record known or believed to be forged.

**Applicability:**
Where forged KYC, offer letters, invoices or electronic records are actually used to deceive — used with S.336 across impersonation/fraud cases.

**Evidence Requirements:**
Certified electronic records under BSA S.63 with S.63(4) certificate; hash values (SHA-256); chain of custody; forensic image of device/server; preservation of original media. Proof the record is forged, that the accused used it as genuine, and knowledge/belief of forgery.

**Related Crime Categories:** Investment Scam, Trading Scam, Social Media Impersonation, Online Shopping Fraud, Identity Theft, Deepfake Fraud, BEC, SIM Swap Fraud, Crypto Fraud, Dark Web Crime, QR Code Fraud, Fake Job Scam

**Investigator Notes:**
Charge alongside S.336: 336 covers making, 340 covers using. Establish the accused's knowledge of forgery.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Charging 340 without proving the user's knowledge/belief of forgery; missing the act of 'use'.

### BNS, 2023 Section 351 — Criminal intimidation

**Section Number:** 351
**Section Name:** Criminal intimidation

**Description:**
Punishes threatening another with injury to person, reputation or property (or to one in whom they are interested) with intent to cause alarm or to compel/deter an act.

**Applicability:**
Loan-app harassment, sextortion threats, ransomware/CSAM coercion, and stalking-related threats.

**Evidence Requirements:**
The threat communications, the intent to alarm/compel, victim statement, and accused attribution.

**Related Crime Categories:** Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud, Ransomware, CSAM

**Investigator Notes:**
Frequently charged with extortion (S.308) where the threat is tied to a demand; preserve original threat content with metadata.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Failing to capture the exact threatening words/medium; not establishing intent to cause alarm or compel.

### BNS, 2023 Section 356 — Defamation

**Section Number:** 356
**Section Name:** Defamation

**Description:**
Defines defamation (harming reputation by spoken/written words, signs or visible representations, including electronic) and prescribes punishment.

**Applicability:**
Reputation attacks via fake profiles, doxxing, deepfakes, and harassment/impersonation campaigns.

**Evidence Requirements:**
The defamatory publication with metadata, proof of publication to third parties, and harm to reputation.

**Related Crime Categories:** Loan App Fraud, Sextortion, Social Media Impersonation, Cyber Stalking & Harassment, Deepfake Fraud

**Investigator Notes:**
Defamation is largely non-cognizable/compoundable — manage victim expectations and procedure accordingly. Preserve the published content before takedown.

**Common Investigation Mistakes:**
Losing the defamatory content to platform takedown before preservation; misjudging the cognizability/procedure of the offence.
